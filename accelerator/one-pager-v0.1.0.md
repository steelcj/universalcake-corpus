## Technological agency through accessibility

**Universal Cake** rebuilds the technology stack to be inclusive from the ground up. Accessibility is not added after — it is the starting point of every design decision.

---

## The Problem

The technology industry designed for a small minority and called it "everyone." Around 5% of the world speaks English natively, yet 54% of websites are in English. 1.3 billion people live with significant disabilities. When every product independently ignores the same people, the cumulative effect is near-total exclusion from the digital infrastructure modern life depends on.

## The Solution

We build accessible themes, websites, and content pipeline tools designed for real-world hardware, bandwidth, and language diversity. Open standards and open formats throughout — no vendor lock-in. User agency as the primary design constraint.

## The Framework

Seven interdependent dimensions — accessibility, sustainability, interoperability, transparency, resilience, human well-being, and knowledge preservation — with accessibility upstream of everything else. Grounded in systems thinking (Meadows), inclusive design research (Treviranus/IDRC), and 25+ years of practice.

## Traction

- Accessible themes, websites, and source archive tools built from the ground up
- Content pipeline supporting user agency across real-world operating systems and hardware
- Framework documented, published, and versioned (v0.3.2)
- Digital sovereignty systems analysis applying Meadows framework
- Experience with university research departments and Canadian disability organisations
- Accessibility-first development since 1999, beginning with Plone (WCAG 1.0 AAA)

## The Market

- **Disability organisations** that need tools built for their communities, not adapted after
- **Government and institutional clients** with multilingual and accessibility mandates
- **Organisations** that need to own their technology stack rather than rent access to it
- Driven by the Accessible Canada Act, Quebec language requirements, and the accelerating sovereignty problem as AI platforms make accessibility decisions without accountability to disability communities
