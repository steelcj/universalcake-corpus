# Defining Vendor Lock-in

Vendor lock-in refers to a situation where a customer becomes so dependent on a specific vendor’s products or services that switching to a competitor becomes prohibitively difficult, costly, or impractical. This dynamic is common in today’s technology landscape, especially with cloud platforms, SaaS (Software as a Service), and proprietary ecosystems.

Key mechanisms enabling vendor lock-in include:

- **Proprietary formats and APIs**: Data or workflows locked into formats that are not easily portable.
- **Integration depth**: Services tightly woven into internal systems, making migration complex.
- **Ecosystem dependency**: Users rely on a suite of interconnected tools (e.g., Google Workspace, Microsoft 365) where replacing one component disrupts others.
- **Cost and effort of migration**: Financial, technical, and operational overhead of moving data, retraining staff, and reconfiguring systems.
- **Lack of interoperability standards**: Absence of universal standards that allow seamless switching between providers.

This dynamic can reduce competition, limit user choice, and increase long-term costs. Ethical and independent providers (like Infomaniak) often emphasize open standards, data portability, and interoperability to mitigate lock-in — aligning with principles of autonomy, privacy, and technological sovereignty.
