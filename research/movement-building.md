## Purpose of This Document

This document focuses on **what to actively do**—not just what to avoid—when building a sustainable movement. These practices are intended to help movements endure **without exhausting people, hollowing values, or collapsing under their own pressure**.

This is not a checklist for perfection.  
It is a **set of stabilizing habits** that, taken together, reduce harm and increase longevity.

---

## Practices That Create a Sustainable Movement

### 1. Pay People for Their Work

- Compensate labor fairly and transparently  
- Budget for salaries, benefits, and raises  
- Treat emotional, administrative, and coordination work as real work  

Pay is not a reward for success—it is **a condition for participation**.

---

### 2. Fund the Work That Keeps the Work Possible

- Allocate resources to administration, care, and infrastructure  
- Invest in documentation, onboarding, and continuity  
- Build reserves when possible  

Sustainability depends on **what happens between moments of action**.

---

### 3. Design for Human Limits

- Normalize rest, time off, and boundaries  
- Plan workloads assuming people are not machines  
- Build redundancy so no one is indispensable  

A movement that only works at maximum output will eventually stop working.

---

### 4. Make Roles Explicit and Negotiable

- Define responsibilities clearly  
- Allow roles to change over time  
- Revisit expectations regularly  

Clarity prevents both burnout and quiet power accumulation.

---

### 5. Rotate Leadership and Share Knowledge

- Avoid concentrating authority in a few people  
- Document decisions, processes, and history  
- Support mentorship and succession  

Movements last longer when **knowledge outlives individuals**.

---

### 6. Treat Care as a Collective Responsibility

- Build peer support into the structure  
- Check in on capacity, not just output  
- Respond to strain early, not after collapse  

Care is not a personal failing—it is **a shared system function**.

---

### 7. Slow Down When Trust Is Required

- Allow time for listening and relationship-building  
- Separate urgency from importance  
- Resist permanent crisis mode  

Some outcomes cannot be rushed without being damaged.

---

### 8. Collaborate Openly and Generously

- Share resources, tools, and learning  
- Coordinate with aligned groups instead of duplicating work  
- Value ecosystem health over organizational growth  

Sustainability increases when movements **stop competing for survival**.

---

### 9. Create Clear Exit and Return Paths

- Make it safe to step back  
- Honor contributions without guilt  
- Support re-entry when people are ready  

People who leave well are more likely to return—or support from elsewhere.

---

### 10. Align Internal Practice With External Values

- Apply dignity, consent, and fairness internally  
- Address harm inside the movement with the same care demanded outside  
- Adjust structures when values and reality diverge  

Credibility is built through **consistency over time**.

---

## A Simple Test

A movement is moving toward sustainability if:

- people stay without burning out,  
- knowledge accumulates instead of disappearing,  
- care increases capacity rather than draining it,  
- and the work remains possible even when conditions worsen.

If these are not true, the solution is rarely “try harder.”  
It is almost always **redesign**.

---

## Closing Reflection

Sustainable movements are not powered by sacrifice.  
They are powered by **care made durable**.

When people are supported, rested, paid, and respected,  
they can keep showing up—not just for a season, but for as long as change requires.

That is not indulgence.  
That is how non-violent change survives.
