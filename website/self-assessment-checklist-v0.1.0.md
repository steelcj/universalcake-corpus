# Universal Cake — Self-Assessment Checklist

Version: 0.1.0
Status: Draft
License: CC BY-SA 4.0

## How to use this checklist

This checklist applies the Universal Cake framework to any technology system — software, hardware, infrastructure, documentation, or communication platform. It is not a compliance tool. It is a thinking tool.

No system will score perfectly across all dimensions. The goal is honest evaluation and a growing commitment to doing better by serving better. Start with accessibility — if people cannot get through the door, nothing else matters.

For each question, note where you stand honestly. The value is in the gaps you discover, not in the score.

---

## 1. Accessibility

*Can people actually use this?*

- [ ] Does this work in the languages of the people who need it?
- [ ] Can people with disabilities use this with assistive technologies?
- [ ] Does this work on older hardware and operating systems?
- [ ] Does this work on low or intermittent bandwidth?
- [ ] Is the cognitive load appropriate for the intended audience?
- [ ] Can individuals and communities afford this over time?
- [ ] Who cannot use this right now — and is that exclusion intentional or a side effect?

**Collapse signal:** If you cannot answer "who cannot use this?", the accessibility gate is closed and you cannot see it.

---

## 2. Sustainability

*Can this last?*

- [ ] Does this burn out the people who maintain it?
- [ ] Does it require constant expensive upgrades to remain functional?
- [ ] What are the environmental costs — energy use, hardware turnover, infrastructure demands?
- [ ] Does it serve the people who depend on it, or does it slowly reduce their agency?
- [ ] Can the organisation sustaining it afford to do so over the next five years? Ten?
- [ ] Are the pacing and workload demands on contributors sustainable over time?

**Collapse signal:** Contributor turnover is accelerating, or maintenance is deferred quarter after quarter.

---

## 3. Interoperability

*Can your data move freely?*

- [ ] Does this use open standards that other tools can read?
- [ ] Can users export their data in a format they can use elsewhere?
- [ ] Can users continue working if they switch to a different tool or platform?
- [ ] Are there hidden dependencies that make switching costly?
- [ ] Does the system lock users in quietly and incrementally?

**Collapse signal:** Users have invested years of work in a format only one vendor can read.

---

## 4. Transparency

*Can you understand what this system is doing?*

- [ ] Can users inspect how the system processes their data?
- [ ] Can the system be audited by someone outside the organisation that built it?
- [ ] Is the agreement with the provider clear about what they can and cannot do?
- [ ] If the system uses AI or automated decision-making, can users understand how decisions are reached?
- [ ] Does the system trust users with information, or demand trust while offering nothing in return?

**Collapse signal:** You cannot explain to a user what the system does with their data.

---

## 5. Resilience

*Can this survive disruption?*

- [ ] What happens if the company behind this closes or is acquired?
- [ ] What happens during an internet outage?
- [ ] What happens if the legal or political environment changes?
- [ ] Can users still access what they have created when things go wrong?
- [ ] If this system disappeared tomorrow, could you recreate its essential function? (The rebuildability test)

**Collapse signal:** The answer to the rebuildability test is no — and nobody has asked the question.

---

## 6. Human Well-being

*Does this support or undermine the people who depend on it?*

- [ ] Does this reduce or increase stress for the people who use it?
- [ ] Does it support healthy habits or undermine them?
- [ ] Does it respect the limited resource of human attention, or extract from it?
- [ ] Does it strengthen or weaken the communities and relationships people depend on?
- [ ] Does it improve one person's experience while degrading the conditions everyone shares?

**Collapse signal:** Users describe the system as something they endure rather than something that serves them.

---

## 7. Knowledge Preservation

*Will your information still be readable in the future?*

- [ ] Will files stored in this system still open in ten years? Twenty?
- [ ] Does the system use open, durable formats or proprietary ones?
- [ ] If the system disappears, does the knowledge stored in it survive?
- [ ] Is knowledge distributed or concentrated in a single point of failure?
- [ ] Can future maintainers understand and improve what has been built?

**Collapse signal:** Critical organisational knowledge exists only inside a system that cannot export it.

---

## Sovereignty Assessment

*Who governs this system?*

These questions apply Chartier's sovereignty framework across your technology stack.

- [ ] Where are decisions about this system actually made — and by whom?
- [ ] If the vendor's interests diverge from yours, who holds authority?
- [ ] Can delegated authority be withdrawn without systemic failure?
- [ ] Do you have the institutional capability to rebuild essential functions independently?
- [ ] Is sovereignty treated as a strategic asset or a compliance checkbox?
- [ ] Are exit paths credible for every critical dependency?
- [ ] Has a rebuildability assessment been conducted in the last twelve months?

**Collapse signal:** The system "works" but you cannot answer who governs it.

---

## Reading Your Results

**Look for patterns, not scores.** The dimensions are interdependent — weakness in one often signals weakness in others. Accessibility problems frequently indicate interoperability and resilience problems. Sovereignty erosion frequently accompanies transparency and knowledge preservation gaps.

**Start with the collapse signals.** If any collapse signal is present, that dimension needs attention before others can improve. The cascade priority applies: protect human capacity first, then open participation, then build knowledge.

**Ask who is missing.** The most important finding is usually not in the answers but in the questions you cannot answer — because the people who would have told you could not get through the door.
