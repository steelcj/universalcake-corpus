# Universal Cake — Glossary of Key Concepts

Status: Draft

## Accessibility

The dimension that asks whether people can actually use a system — in their language, on their device, with their abilities, on their bandwidth, at a cost they can sustain. In Universal Cake, accessibility is not a feature to add later. It is the door to the house. Without it, nothing else on the list matters. Accessibility is upstream of all other dimensions.

## Abstraction Spiral

A reinforcing loop (R2) in the digital sovereignty system. Abstraction is adopted to manage complexity, but it also reduces observability. Reduced observability makes the system harder to govern, which increases effective complexity, which generates demand for further abstraction. The system becomes simultaneously more opaque and more dependent on the abstractions that created the opacity.

## Balancing Loop

A feedback loop that stabilises a system by resisting change away from a target state. Universal Cake identifies three: clear standards reduce drift (B1), modular infrastructure limits maintenance accumulation (B2), and sustainable pacing prevents burnout (B3). Without these balancing loops, the reinforcing loops overshoot or collapse.

## Bridging Capital

The social resources built by forming relationships across social boundaries, as distinct from bonding capital, which strengthens existing relationships (Putnam, 2000). Organisations that invest in the edges rather than only harvesting the centre build bridging capital, which expands the pool of participants, increases diversity of perspectives, and enables better exploration (March, 1991).

## Capability Erosion

A reinforcing loop (R3) in which dependency reduces the internal need to maintain independent capability. Skills atrophy. Documentation lapses. Institutional knowledge depletes. As it depletes, the cost of reducing dependency increases, making further dependency the rational choice. The organisation becomes more dependent precisely because it is dependent.

## Cascade Priority

The natural startup sequence for Universal Cake's reinforcing loops, derived from the principle that a reinforcing loop cannot run faster than its slowest stock allows. The sequence is: protect human capacity first (B3), then open participation inflows (R1 + R2), then build knowledge. Skip a step and the cascade collapses.

## Compounded Digital Exclusion

The phenomenon in which digital inequalities do not operate along independent axes but overlap, interlink, and interact, producing outcomes far more severe than any single exclusion would suggest (Zheng & Walsham, 2021). When every product independently ignores 20% of its potential users, the same people lose access to nearly everything.

## Convenience Trap

The most pervasive reinforcing loop (R1) in the digital sovereignty system. Convenience adoption builds dependency. Dependency makes alternatives appear more expensive. This increases the attractiveness of further adoption within the same ecosystem. Each step makes exit harder. Each step makes the next step more likely. Sovereignty is lost through accumulation, not through any single decision.

## Ecotone Effect

From ecology: the transitional zone between two ecosystems produces the greatest species richness and capabilities found nowhere else (Odum, 1953). Applied to technology and communities: the comfortable centre of any system is well-optimised but fragile. The edges have been asked to cope and have developed capabilities the centre never needed. Innovation lives at the edge.

## Human Capacity

The stock of energy, attention, trust, and motivation that contributors bring to a system. It is finite and renewable — but only if the drain rate stays within bounds. It is the most fundamental stock in the Universal Cake system. All other loops feed into or draw from it. If human capacity is below threshold, no virtuous cascade can sustain itself.

## Human Well-being

The dimension that asks how a system affects the people who use it and the world those people are part of. Well-being is not a feature. It is a state that emerges from the quality of relationships — with our bodies, our minds, and the people, technology, and world around us. A product that improves one person's experience while degrading the conditions everyone shares is not a well-being gain. It is a transfer.

## Interoperability

The dimension that asks whether data can move freely and whether the user can leave. Does the system use open standards? Or does it lock users in, quietly, incrementally, until switching becomes too costly to consider?

## Knowledge Preservation

The dimension that asks whether information stored in a system will remain readable in the future. Will files still open in twenty years? Does the system depend on a proprietary format that could disappear and take everything stored in it?

## Language Infrastructure

The structural layer that determines which communities can communicate within and through a system. Includes translation coverage, plain language standards, multilingual documentation, and the assumptions baked into interfaces about what a "default" user reads and thinks in. Universal Cake treats language as load-bearing infrastructure — not cosmetic, not optional, but foundational. Weak language infrastructure structurally excludes entire communities.

## Minority Stress

The chronic, socially-based stress experienced by marginalised groups through stigma, discrimination, and exclusion, producing measurable health disparities (Meyer, 2003). The biopsychosocial effects of social stress shape rates of biological aging through inflammatory processes (Brown et al., 2020). Exclusion makes people sick. This is not metaphor. It is measurable physiology.

## Observability

The ability to see what a system is actually doing at runtime. Sovereignty exists in execution, not in documentation — a system that appears sovereign in design may behave differently under real conditions. Without continuous observation, drift from intended governance is undetectable until it becomes severe.

## Rebuildability Test

A governance gate proposed by Chartier (2026): "If this system disappeared tomorrow, could we recreate its essential function ourselves?" If the answer is no, you do not own what you depend on. Applied regularly and honestly, this question converts invisible dependency into a visible, assessable signal.

## Reinforcing Loop

A feedback loop that amplifies change in whichever direction it runs — virtuous or vicious. Universal Cake's two primary reinforcing loops are the accessibility loop (R1: accessibility → participation → contributors → documentation → accessibility) and the language infrastructure loop (R2: language infrastructure → community engagement → diverse knowledge → system strength → language infrastructure).

## Resilience

The dimension that asks whether a system can survive disruption — a company closing, an internet outage, a change in the legal or political environment. Are users still able to access what they have created when things go wrong?

## Sovereignty

Legitimate authority — the recognised right to make decisions, enforce them, and be held accountable for their consequences (Chartier, 2025–2026). Distinct from power, which is merely the ability to act. A system that makes decisions without oversight is not sovereign. It is simply powerful. Organisations can simultaneously possess enormous technical capability and almost no sovereign authority over that capability.

## Sovereignty Dividend

A positive reinforcing loop (R5): governance investment builds sovereign authority, which enables better observability, which enables effective enforcement, which reinforces confidence in further governance investment. This loop does not self-start. It requires the deliberate decision to treat sovereignty as a strategic objective rather than a compliance outcome.

## Sustainability

The dimension that asks whether a system can last. Includes environmental costs, but also human costs: does it burn out its maintainers? Does it require constant expensive upgrades? Does it slowly reduce user agency and consume resources, offering only short-term returns?

## Transparency

The dimension that asks whether users can understand what a system is doing and what the agreement with the provider actually is. Can it be inspected? Audited? Or is it a black box that demands trust while offering nothing in return?

## Virtuous Tornado

A concept from Jutta Treviranus (2019). The continuous, iterative process of asking "who are we missing?" and expanding inclusive design practices to reach them. Creates systems that grow stronger and more capable with each cycle. The people at the margins cover 80% of the knowledge terrain — designing for them repays in knowledge, understanding, and systems that reach the edges of human diversity.
