# Universal Cake vocabulary - Universal Cake Cascades

This is a good idea and it has real systems grounding — it's not just a metaphor.

------

**On the tornado question first.** Meadows would not use that word but the phenomenon she would use is *co-reinforcement between loops*. When two reinforcing loops share a node or a stock, they do not simply add their effects — they multiply them. R1 and R2 both draw from the Human Capacity stock and both build the Participation stock. When both loops are running in their virtuous direction simultaneously, the shared stocks build faster than either loop could drive alone. The loops amplify each other rather than running in parallel. That is structurally distinct from a single loop accelerating, and it is what your tornado intuition is pointing at.

Whether to call it a tornado depends on what you want the concept to do. A tornado implies a single vortex — everything spiraling into one center. What's actually happening here is more like two weather systems that strengthen each other when they overlap. There may be a better term inside the Universal Cake vocabulary.

------

**On priority and sequencing — this is the more important question.** Yes, cascades have a natural order, and Meadows gives you the framework to identify it.

The logic is: *a reinforcing loop cannot run faster than its slowest stock allows.* Stocks have inertia. You cannot will a stock to a higher level — you have to build it through flows, and flows take time.

> This means the cascade that builds the foundational stock first creates the conditions for all subsequent cascades to run faster and more stably.

Applied to Universal Cake, the priority sequence falls out of the stock analysis already done:

**Human Capacity must come first.** It has the longest delay dynamics, the most severe threshold behaviour, and is the stock every other loop draws from. B3 (Sustainable Pacing) is therefore not one balancing loop among three equals — it is a prerequisite. If Human Capacity is below threshold, no virtuous cascade can sustain itself regardless of how well designed everything else is. Contributors will exit before the loops can compound.

**Participation second.** Once Human Capacity is protected, Participation is the load-bearing stock for both R1 and R2. Building Participation — through accessible entry points and language coverage — activates both reinforcing loops. Without sufficient Participation, R1 and R2 are structurally present but too weak to self-sustain.

**Knowledge third.** Not because it is unimportant but because it is the most recoverable of the three stocks given healthy Human Capacity and Participation. A community with contributors and engaged diverse communities can rebuild a degraded Knowledge stock. The reverse is not true.

This gives you a cascade priority: **B3 → Participation inflows (R1 + R2 entry nodes) → Knowledge inflows**. Or in plain language: sustain the people first, then open the doors, then build what they produce together.

------

**On how this fits Meadows' framework.** She does not name this phenomenon explicitly but it is consistent with two things she does address.

The first is *prerequisites for loop activation*. A reinforcing loop that lacks adequate stock to draw from produces positive feedback that is too weak to be self-sustaining — it grows for a while and then stalls or reverses when the stock drops below the minimum needed to keep the loop running. She calls this the difference between a loop that is *active* and one that is *dominant*. The cascade priority question is really a question about what conditions make the virtuous loops dominant rather than just nominally present.

The second is *limiting factors*. In any system with multiple reinforcing loops, the overall growth rate is limited by whichever stock is most depleted. This is Liebig's law of the minimum applied to sociotechnical systems — you do not get the combined amplification of multiple virtuous loops if one stock is below its operating threshold. The cascade that addresses the most limiting stock first produces the largest total gain.

The detail layer is the theory. This is where theory becomes sequence.
