# Universal Cake

## Accessibility & Sovereignty

*A framework that asks how the tools we build together can best serve human agency.*

---

# What is Universal Cake?

Universal Cake is a framework for evaluating technology in human terms.

It asks a different question than the industry default.

**Industry default:** Does it work? Is it fast? Is it cheap?

**Universal Cake:** Does it work *in a way that supports the humans who depend on it*?

It is not a checklist. It is an attempt to change what technology is for.

---

# Origin

In 1999, Christopher Steel needed website software for a community that included:

- People with disabilities
- People who did not read or write English
- People using older hardware and operating systems

He wrote a checklist of qualities that mattered most.

**Accessibility moved to the top** — not as a political statement, but as a logical one.

> Without the door, the rest of the house is irrelevant.

---

# The Core Shift

Most technology evaluation optimises for performance measured against the immediate needs of whoever is already being served.

Universal Cake proposes a different goal:

> Technology that **builds, replenishes, and bridges** rather than **extracts, consumes, and diminishes**.

This is not a feature request. It is a change to the goal function — to the question the whole system is organised to answer.

---

# Seven Dimensions

Universal Cake evaluates technology across seven interdependent dimensions:

| Dimension | Core question |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Accessibility** | Can people actually use this? |
| **Sustainability** | Can this last? |
| **Interoperability** | Can your data move freely? |
| **Transparency** | Can you understand what this system is doing? |
| **Resilience** | Can this survive disruption? |
| **Human well-being** | How does this affect people and the world they share? |
| **Knowledge preservation** | Will your information remain readable in the future? |

These are not a flat list. They are a map of dependencies.

---

# Accessibility Is Upstream

**Accessibility is not one dimension among seven. It is upstream of everything else.**

Without it, the other dimensions cannot be reached at all.

- Interoperability enables resilience
- Transparency enables sustainability
- Knowledge preservation depends on both
- Human well-being is the purpose that gives the other six their meaning

**But none of them matter if people cannot get through the door.**

# Part I: Accessibility

*Who are we missing?*

---

# The 80/20 Problem

The technology industry designed for a small minority of the world, gave that minority a name — "the majority" — and then applied a rule about Italian land ownership from the 1890s to justify ignoring everyone else.

**The actual numbers:**

- ~380 million people speak English natively (~5% of world population)
- 54% of websites worldwide are in English
- 7,170 living languages; 44% now endangered
- More than 4 in 5 people do not understand English

The "80%" was never the world's 80%. It was the 80% of a narrow market.

---

# Compounded Digital Exclusion

When every product independently ignores 20%, **the same people lose everything**.

A person who speaks a minority language, lives in a low-income household, uses older hardware, has low bandwidth, and has a visual impairment is not excluded from one thing.

**They are excluded from nearly everything.**

- The banking app requires current hardware
- The government portal is only in the dominant language
- The healthcare platform needs a screen resolution they cannot produce
- The job search tool assumes broadband

> Each decision was "reasonable." The cumulative effect is near-total exclusion.

---

# The Cost of Exclusion

**Exclusion is measurable, not abstract:**

- Economic losses from excluding persons with disabilities: **3-7% of GDP** (ILO, 2009)
- People are less productive not because they are "disabled" but because they live in environments that are **disabling**
- Chronic social stress shapes rates of biological aging through inflammatory processes (Brown et al., 2020)

> The cost is not in the people. **It is in the systems that exclude them.**

---

# Innovation Lives at the Edge

**The ecotone effect:** Transitional zones between ecosystems produce the greatest species richness and capabilities found nowhere else (Odum, 1953).

**Treviranus (2019):** The people at the margins cover 80% of the knowledge terrain. Designing for them repays you in knowledge, understanding, and systems that reach the edges of human diversity.

**The Virtuous Tornado:** A continuous, iterative process of asking *who are we missing* and expanding inclusive design to reach them creates systems that grow stronger with each cycle.

> The comfortable centre is well-optimised. It is also the most fragile.

---

# The Accessibility Reinforcing Loop

When this loop runs forward, accessibility compounds. When it collapses, exclusion compounds.

```
Accessibility → expands → Participation
Participation → attracts → Contributors
Contributors → improves → Documentation
Documentation → strengthens → Accessibility
```

**Collapse direction:** Inaccessible systems → fewer participants → fewer contributors → degraded documentation → more inaccessible systems.

Most technology systems are experiencing this collapse without recognising it as structural.

---

# Language as Infrastructure

Language is not cosmetic. It is load-bearing — like roads and power grids.

```
Language Infrastructure → enables → Community Engagement
Community Engagement → generates → Diverse Knowledge
Diverse Knowledge → strengthens → System Strength
System Strength → invests in → Language Infrastructure
```

**Collapse direction:** Weak language infrastructure → limited engagement → homogeneous knowledge → fragile system → less investment in language.

Most systems launched in one language, optimised for one community, and produced knowledge that reflects only that community's experience.

---

# The Cascade Priority

Systems thinking reveals a natural startup sequence:

1. **Protect human capacity first** — sustain the people
2. **Open participation inflows** — open the doors
3. **Build knowledge** — build what people produce together

> A reinforcing loop cannot run faster than its slowest stock allows.

Human capacity must be protected before participation can build. Participation must build before knowledge can grow. Skip a step and the cascade collapses.

# Part II: Digital Sovereignty

*Who governs the systems that make decisions on our behalf?*

---

# What Sovereignty Actually Is

Sovereignty is **legitimate authority** — the recognised right to make decisions, enforce them, and be held accountable for their consequences.

This is distinct from **power**, which is merely the ability to act.

> A system that makes decisions without oversight is not sovereign. It is simply powerful.

**The critical insight:** Organisations can simultaneously possess enormous technical capability and almost no sovereign authority over that capability.

*Source: Roy Chartier, State of Mind series (2025-2026)*

---

# How Sovereignty Is Lost

Sovereignty is not lost through dramatic decisions. It is lost through **accumulation**.

> "Incrementally through a series of rational, well-intentioned decisions made under pressure to modernise, scale, or simplify operations."

**The experience of using a system feels identical whether authority is retained or surrendered.**

No single event announces the transition from governance to dependency. No alarm fires. The capability loss is only revealed when the vendor relationship changes.

---

# The Convenience Trap (R1)

The most pervasive reinforcing loop in digital sovereignty:

```
Convenience adoption → Dependency accumulates →
Alternatives appear more expensive →
Further convenience adoption within same ecosystem →
(repeat)
```

Each step makes exit harder. Each step makes the next step more likely.

> "Convenience becomes the mechanism through which control quietly leaves the room."

The loop produces abdication through accumulation. No single decision relinquishes sovereignty.

---

# The Erosion Loops

Four reinforcing loops accelerate sovereignty erosion:

| Loop | Mechanism |
|------|-----------|
| **R1: Convenience Trap** | Convenience → dependency → more convenience |
| **R2: Abstraction Spiral** | Abstraction reduces observability → more abstraction needed |
| **R3: Capability Erosion** | Dependency → skills atrophy → can't reduce dependency |
| **R4: Market Concentration** | Scale creates scale — no internal limiting mechanism |

All four are self-reinforcing. None require an explicit decision to relinquish sovereignty.

**The balancing loops (crisis, regulation) activate only after significant damage.**

---

# The Delays That Trap Us

The system is structurally biased against sovereign behaviour:

| | Benefits | Costs |
|---|---------|-------|
| **Dependency** | Immediate and visible | Deferred and invisible |
| **Sovereignty investment** | Deferred and invisible | Immediate and visible |

> "Much like insurance, sovereignty often appears expensive until the day it becomes necessary."

This asymmetry means sovereignty is systematically underfunded in organisations that optimise for near-term efficiency.

---

# System Traps

Four predictable failure patterns operate regardless of intention:

**Fixes That Backfire:** Managed services reduce operational burden but also reduce observability and deplete institutional capability.

**Shifting the Burden:** Outsourced complexity transfers capability to an external actor. The burden is not solved — it is compounded.

**Tragedy of the Commons:** Individual rationality produces collective vulnerability. Each rational adoption of dominant platforms depletes the commons of alternatives.

**Success to the Successful:** The market concentration flywheel has no internal resolution mechanism.

---

# The Sovereignty Dividend (R5)

A positive reinforcing loop — if it can be initiated:

```
Governance investment → Sovereign authority →
Better observability → Effective enforcement →
Confidence in further governance investment →
(repeat)
```

**This loop does not self-start.** It requires deliberate initiation.

The initiating condition: the decision to treat sovereignty as a **strategic objective** rather than a compliance outcome.

---

# Where to Intervene

Leverage points ranked from least to most powerful (Meadows, 1999):

| Level | Intervention |
|-------|-------------|
| **Parameters** | Compliance thresholds, procurement scoring |
| **Feedback loops** | Runtime monitoring, reversibility requirements, rebuildability assessment |
| **Information flows** | Authority visibility mapping, seven-question sovereignty audit |
| **Rules** | Control plane sovereignty as non-negotiable; revocability of delegation as design rule |
| **Goals** | Reframe from efficiency to resilience; sovereign capability as strategic asset |
| **Paradigms** | Compliance → governance; static → runtime sovereignty; vendor relationship → authority structure |

Most current responses operate at the parameter level. **The leverage is higher.**

---

# Three Paradigm Shifts

The deepest and most powerful interventions:

**1. From compliance to governance**
Compliance produces failure: "Systems may comply with formal requirements while still embedding external authority." Replace with: what questions get asked, what gets measured, what counts as success.

**2. From static to runtime sovereignty**
Sovereignty cannot be declared once and left unattended. It must be actively maintained as the system evolves. Continuous enforcement is a core operational function.

**3. From vendor relationship to authority structure**
Every platform adoption decision is simultaneously a governance decision: who holds authority over a critical function when interests diverge?

---

# The Rebuildability Test

A single question that converts invisible erosion into a visible signal:

> "If this system disappeared tomorrow, could we recreate its essential function ourselves?"

**If the answer is no, you do not own what you depend on.**

Applied regularly and honestly, this question:

- Limits dependency accumulation at the point of adoption
- Creates friction before crisis rather than after
- Converts a latent balancing mechanism into an active governance gate

> "You don't own what you can't rebuild."

# Part III: Where Accessibility and Sovereignty Meet

---

# Two Sides of the Same System

Accessibility and sovereignty are not separate concerns. They are structurally connected.

**Accessibility asks:** Who can participate in the system?
**Sovereignty asks:** Who governs the system?

Both are eroded by the same forces:
- Convenience-driven decisions that defer costs
- Concentration of power in the centre
- Invisible, incremental degradation
- Optimisation for the short list at the expense of the full picture

**A system that excludes people at the door and surrenders authority to external actors is neither accessible nor sovereign.**

---

# The Shared Pattern

Both accessibility and sovereignty share the same structural dynamics:

| Dynamic | Accessibility | Sovereignty |
|---------|--------------|-------------|
| **Erosion is invisible** | Exclusion accumulates without signals | Authority depletes without alarms |
| **Delays mask damage** | Contributor loss appears months later | Capability atrophy reveals itself only in crisis |
| **Reinforcing loops** | Exclusion compounds exclusion | Dependency compounds dependency |
| **Benefits of investment are deferred** | Participation builds slowly | Governance capacity builds slowly |
| **Centre feels fine** | The "majority" is well-served | The system "works" |
| **Edge reveals the truth** | Who cannot get in? | Who actually governs? |

---

# What Universal Cake Asks

The guiding questions, starting with accessibility:

- **Who can use this? Who cannot?**
- Does it work in the languages of the people who need it?
- Will this still work in ten years?
- Can I take my data with me if I leave?
- Can I understand what this system is doing?
- Does this system support or undermine wellbeing?
- What happens if the organisation behind it disappears?
- Does it work for people with different abilities, languages, and devices?
- **Who is still being left out?**

> These questions shift the focus from "what can this do?" to "what does this do to people, and to the world they share?"

---

# The Shift

From capability to consequence.
From the centre of the bell curve to its edges.
From the short list to the full picture.

> Technology that **builds, replenishes, and bridges** rather than **extracts, consumes, and diminishes**.

Universal Cake does not reject performance, speed, or cost.

It places them within the context of our real world — the world in which humans, our communities, and our natural resources are found.

**That shift is the heart of Universal Cake.**

---

# Next Steps

**For assessment:**
- Apply the seven-question sovereignty audit to your own stack
- Audit your system through the eyes of a first-time participant
- Ask the rebuildability question for every critical dependency

**For design:**
- Treat accessibility as participation infrastructure, not compliance
- Treat language as load-bearing infrastructure
- Embed revocability and exit paths before dependencies are accepted

**For culture:**
- Sustain the people first, then open the doors, then build together
- Shift from efficiency to resilience as the organising goal
- Hold the full picture — not just the short list
