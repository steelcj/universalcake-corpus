# The Cascade Priority: What to Build First, and Why

Version: 0.1.0
Status: Draft
License: CC BY-SA 4.0

## Abstract

Universal Cake's reinforcing loops do not all start at once. Systems thinking reveals a natural startup sequence — a cascade priority — that determines what must come first if the loops are to run in their virtuous direction. This guide explains that sequence and why skipping a step collapses the cascade.

---

## The principle

A reinforcing loop cannot run faster than its slowest stock allows.

Stocks have inertia. You cannot will a stock to a higher level — you have to build it through flows, and flows take time. The cascade that builds the foundational stock first creates the conditions for all subsequent cascades to run faster and more stably.

This is Liebig's law of the minimum applied to sociotechnical systems: in any system with multiple reinforcing loops, the overall growth rate is limited by whichever stock is most depleted. The cascade that addresses the most limiting stock first produces the largest total gain.

---

## The sequence

### 1. Protect human capacity first

Human capacity — the stock of energy, attention, trust, and motivation that contributors bring — has the longest delay dynamics, the most severe threshold behaviour, and is the stock every other loop draws from.

The balancing loop B3 (sustainable pacing) is not one loop among three equals. It is a prerequisite. If human capacity is below threshold, no virtuous cascade can sustain itself regardless of how well designed everything else is. Contributors will exit before the loops can compound.

Human capacity builds through rest, meaningful work, and distributed responsibility. It drains through overload, exclusion, and structural misalignment. Because it changes slowly, the delays between causes and effects are long — making it easy to deplete before the problem registers.

**Burnout is not a personal failure. It is the predictable output of a system that drains human capacity faster than it can be replenished.**

Practical implications:
- Design work rates that contributors can maintain over time
- Distribute responsibility rather than concentrating it
- Reduce cognitive load through clear standards and modular infrastructure
- Recognise that burned-out contributors do not recover quickly — prevention is far cheaper than repair

### 2. Open participation

Once human capacity is protected, participation is the load-bearing stock for both reinforcing loops. Building participation — through accessible entry points and language coverage — activates both the accessibility loop (R1) and the language infrastructure loop (R2).

Without sufficient participation, R1 and R2 are structurally present but too weak to self-sustain. The loops exist on paper. They do not run in practice.

Opening participation means:
- Lowering accessibility barriers — language, device, bandwidth, ability, cognitive load
- Building language infrastructure before expecting multilingual engagement
- Creating contribution paths that do not require insider knowledge
- Treating accessibility as participation infrastructure, not compliance

The accessibility gate and the language gate must both be open for the reinforcing loops to activate. Open one without the other and participation remains narrow.

### 3. Build knowledge

Knowledge — documentation quality, accessible standards, translatable content — is the most recoverable of the three stocks given healthy human capacity and participation. A community with contributors and engaged diverse communities can rebuild a degraded knowledge stock. The reverse is not true — a system with excellent documentation but no contributors to maintain it will watch that documentation decay.

This is why knowledge comes third, not because it is unimportant but because it depends on the two stocks that precede it.

Building knowledge means:
- Documenting in the languages of the people who will use it
- Using open, durable formats that survive system changes
- Maintaining documentation as a continuous flow, not a one-time effort
- Treating documentation quality as a structural input to accessibility

---

## Why the sequence matters

**Skip step one and contributors burn out before the loops compound.** The system launches with energy and optimism. Unsustainable pacing depletes human capacity. Contributors leave. The reinforcing loops stall. Knowledge degrades. The system enters collapse direction.

**Skip step two and knowledge serves only the centre.** The system documents well but for a narrow audience. Participation never broadens. Diverse knowledge never develops. The system becomes brittle — well-documented but unable to adapt to conditions outside its narrow design parameters.

**Follow the sequence and the loops amplify each other.** Protected human capacity sustains contributors. Open participation activates the reinforcing loops. Knowledge builds from diverse perspectives. The system grows stronger with each cycle.

---

## Co-reinforcement

When the accessibility loop (R1) and the language infrastructure loop (R2) are both running in their virtuous direction simultaneously, they share stocks — both draw from human capacity and both build participation. When both loops share a node or a stock, they do not simply add their effects. They multiply them. The shared stocks build faster than either loop could drive alone.

This is structurally distinct from a single loop accelerating. It is the amplification effect that Treviranus (2019) describes as the Virtuous Tornado — systems that grow stronger with each cycle because multiple reinforcing loops are strengthening each other.

The cascade priority is the startup sequence that makes this co-reinforcement possible. Without it, the loops either never activate or run so weakly that they cannot sustain themselves.

---

## In plain language

Sustain the people first. Then open the doors. Then build what they produce together.
